Biodex Guide

4 ways to help prevent costly Biodex repairs and reduce downtime.

Small habits that keep isokinetic systems out of the repair queue.

By Chris Senzamici, owner and technician, Precision MedTech Services · Download the PDF

Expensive Biodex repairs rarely start out expensive. Most begin as a small problem, a habit repeated every day, or a service mistake. After 30 years servicing Biodex equipment, these are the four I see most often, and all four are easy to prevent.

1. Fix loose signal cable strain reliefs

The signal cable runs up the dynamometer’s gimbal post. If its strain relief works loose, the cable can slip into the moving assembly, where it gets pinched or cut. A damaged signal cable can short and take out a circuit board, which turns a five-minute fix into a costly repair.

What to do: If the strain relief is loose or the cable is sitting anywhere it shouldn’t, stop and have it secured promptly.

2. Don’t overtighten the tilt or rotation locks

The tilt and rotation locks hold position by gear teeth engaging, not by friction. Cranking them down harder adds no holding power. It only stresses the mechanism, and over time that stress can break a weld, which means replacing the part.

What to do: Train staff to lock them firmly, not forcefully. If a lock won’t hold at normal pressure, that’s a service call, not a reason to push harder.

3. Use the trolley release pedals correctly

Pressing the edge or end of a trolley release pedal twists the mechanism. Done repeatedly, it causes gradual wear that eventually shows up as a pedal that sticks or won’t release.

What to do: Press with your full foot, on or near the center of the pedal.

4. Use only experienced technicians

An inexperienced technician may take apart major components that didn’t need to come apart, or replace parts while chasing the wrong fault. That turns a simple repair into a long, expensive one, and adds downtime.

What to do: Choose a technician with specific Biodex experience who diagnoses first and replaces parts second. Learn more about independent Biodex repair.

The bottom line

A little attention in these four areas reduces avoidable repairs, keeps equipment available for patients, extends its life and keeps service costs under control. Regular preventive maintenance catches the rest.
